00 · Pillar

The rule every decision answers to

One constraint shapes the whole balance: since the game cannot modify the player's speed, no penalty and no bonus may act on speed.

Every modifier has to be expressed in a different currency — metabolic cost, heart rate, accuracy, injury risk or the time an action takes. The constraint is hard, there is no way around it, and every system on this page is derived from it.

What we deliberately do not model

Fractures, infections, illness, body temperature as a separate parameter, food poisoning and mental health. The scope is limited on purpose — the model is meant to be computable, not complete.

1:1time scale — the day is never sped up
0game servers — the whole simulation runs locally
0in-app purchases
0health data collected
01 · Character

A creator that actually computes

You enter sex, age, height and weight. The game turns them into physiological parameters using clinical formulas rather than a table of character classes.

Formulas

Blood volume — Nadler M V = 0.3669 × h³ + 0.03219 × w + 0.6041 F V = 0.3561 × h³ + 0.03308 × w + 0.1833 Basal metabolic rate — Mifflin–St Jeor M BMR = 10w + 6.25h − 5a + 5 F BMR = 10w + 6.25h − 5a − 161 Water = 35 ml × body mass + sweat losses Max HR = 208 − 0.7 × age (Tanaka) Carry load = 0.30 × mass (comfort) · 0.45 × mass (hard limit)

Validation

  • Age 16–80, height 120–220 cm, weight 35–200 kg
  • Cross-validation rejects combinations below BMI 12 or above 60 — without it a player enters 200 cm / 35 kg and every formula falls apart
  • Name of 4–16 alphanumeric characters with spaces, no leading or doubled spaces
Privacy

Height, weight, age and sex are health-related data. They stay on the device and never leave the phone. The game does not integrate with Health Connect or any sensor — the simplest way to keep that promise is to not collect the data at all.

Exceeding the comfortable carry load does not block movement — we cannot slow a real person down. It scales the metabolic cost instead.

Metabolic zones

Metabolic rate depends on where the character is, not on whether the app process is running. Resting under a roof genuinely lowers consumption, so no fiction is needed.

A simple rule follows, and it never has to be spelled out: end your session under a roof. Quitting the app in the open costs three times as much as quitting in the shelter.

Safety valve

Offline consumption never drops any resource below 10% and never causes death. Coming back from a two-week holiday should hurt, not delete your character. Rolling the system clock back yields a zero-length tick, never a negative one.

Heart rate as hidden stamina

There is no stamina bar, but heart rate fills that role functionally — and it drives accuracy.

Heart rate as % of maxEffect

Recovery to resting rate is exponential with a time constant of roughly 90 seconds. That number is why you cannot “wait for your heart to slow down” once something is already running at you.

Sleep is the default state, not an action

The character sleeps when three conditions hold at once: inside the shelter or camp zone, night has fallen (derived from sunrise and sunset computed offline), and no other occupation is running. The player taps nothing.

In summer you must choose between sleep and progress. Sleep debt raises dispersion and lengthens every action, so “I'll read instead of sleeping” carries a price paid the next day.

One occupation at a time

The character cannot sleep, read and build simultaneously. This is the main mechanism of the game's time economy — without it every shelter system would run in parallel and none would have a cost.

Damage and bleeding

Haemorrhagic shock thresholds follow the ATLS classes rather than abstract hit points.

Blood lossClassPlayer state
< 15%Ino symptoms

Running with an open wound accelerates bleeding — at a heart rate of 160 against a resting 70, exactly 2.3 times. Bleeding tiers: superficial 3 ml/min, moderate 25, severe 90, arterial 350 ml/min and a tourniquet is the only answer.

03 · Map and GPS

Your city is the board

The map is real and downloaded once as an offline package. The game has no servers, so the tiles have to be self-sufficient and the GPS stream filtered before anything is computed from it.

PMTilesone file per region, Protomaps + MapLibre GL Native
50–200 MBtile package per province
25 maccuracy threshold for discarding a fix
8 m / 10 sdead zone — below this there is no movement

Signal quality

Urban GPS accuracy is 5–15 m and indoors there is no signal at all. Position drift on a stationary player would generate phantom movement and burn calories for someone sitting on a sofa.

  • Kalman filter on the position stream
  • Fixes with accuracy worse than 25 m are discarded
  • Displacement under 8 m in 10 s counts as no movement
  • Signal loss beyond 60 s pauses movement simulation and tells the player

Battery

Continuous GPS costs 15–25% of battery per hour — a real threat to multi-hour play. The answer is adaptive sampling: 1 Hz in combat, 0.2 Hz while walking, 0.05 Hz at rest, plus a foreground service with a persistent notification and a power-saving mode with a reduced map.

Position anti-cheat

Without a server, full anti-cheat is impossible and we accept that — the game is single-player, so a cheater only harms themselves. The minimum is mock location detection and a speed check: above 40 km/h for more than 30 s the session is suspended, not punished.

04 · Safety

The game sends people onto the street after dark

This is not an optional section. The lessons from location-based games apply directly here, and skipping them would be a legal and reputational risk.

Spawn exclusion zones

Filtered by OpenStreetMap tags before any enemy or loot point appears.

  • Carriageways and roadsides of major roads — 15 m buffer
  • Railway tracks — 30 m buffer
  • Water bodies and watercourses
  • Private land and residential buildings tagged access=private
  • Hospitals, schools, kindergartens, cemeteries, places of worship, police stations, military sites

Protective mechanics

  • Combat is blocked above 15 km/h with the message “Do not play while driving”
  • A one-time screen requiring active acceptance of the safety rules before the first session
  • After local sunset, a reminder about visibility and situational awareness
  • The game never requires headphones; every critical signal has a haptic and visual duplicate
05 · Items

Two limits: mass and volume

Weight alone is not enough — without volume a player fits thirty kilograms of down jackets in a pocket. No per-item count caps; the constraint comes purely from physics.

06 · Combat

You are not fighting a random number generator

MOA describes angular dispersion, not probability. Hit chance follows from target geometry and a sum of errors dominated by the shooter's bodily state — not by character level.

The conclusion reshapes the design: a calm, stationary shooter hits reliably — which is exactly how it works in reality. Difficulty does not come from the ballistic model but from the fact that the game almost never grants the conditions of calm and stillness. A trained shooter on the run is as helpless as a novice.

Interface requirement

Above the fire button the game shows the numeric hit chance and the largest current error contributor: “MOTION”, “HEART RATE”, “DISTANCE”. The model may be as merciless as it is transparent — without that number, a player who misses five times concludes the game is broken.

Damage

loss [ml] = 5.1 × energy[J]^0.6 × wound_factor × location_multiplier × (1 − protection)

The relationship is a power law below one, because wound severity does not scale linearly with energy. wound_factor captures the character of the wound channel: rifle rounds fragment and cavitate, buckshot creates multiple channels, pistol rounds pass straight through.

Fighting a group

Shamblers move in groups of 2–4, so fighting several at once is the default state. A firearm has one active target; switching costs 1.2 s during which the dispersion circle is at its widest. There is no automatic switch after a target dies — automation would remove all the tension and turn combat into tapping.

Time, not ammunition, is the main resource here. The group detects you, all of them sprint, and every second spent aiming is metres covered by the rest. When their sprint budgets run out, a window opens for reloading, withdrawing or finishing off the exhausted.

08 · Enemies

Three stages of one disease

The player's speed cannot be modified, so a fast enemy would be a death sentence. The answer is a sprint budget — the dread stays, escape remains possible.

TypeWalkRunSprint budgetBloodDetection
Leaper5–7 km/h27–32 km/h25 s2,400–2,800 ml120 m
Shambler3–4 km/h15–18 km/h90 s3,200–3,600 ml80 m
Brute2–4 km/h12–17 km/h45 s6,000–8,000 ml60 m

Sprinters do not hold top speed longer than 20–30 seconds — that is physiology, not a concession. The player survives the sprint and exploits the human endurance advantage. There is no outrunning a Leaper and that is its design role; Shamblers and Brutes can be escaped.

State machine

StateEntry conditionBehaviour
Idledefaultrandom movement within the hotspot

In Chase and Exhausted the enemy never stops. Combat is never static — shooting one means losing distance to all the others. The 400 m leash and the 45-second contact-loss rule stop you from dragging a train of enemies across half the city.

The three types are three stages of the same disease, which explains their parameters without changing a single number: the Leaper was infected earliest and is metabolically wasted, Shamblers came with the second wave and move in groups because they were infected together, and the Brute is the latest stage — oedema and fluid retention.

09 · Hotspots

The clock that measures out difficulty

A contamination hotspot is a permanent point on the map that spawns enemies. It grows over time — including while you are not playing. This is the direct expression of the goal “survive as long as possible”: the longer the streak, the greater the pressure.

Neutralisation

Integrity_max(level) = 60 + 20 × level // 80 pts at level 1, 260 at level 10 Shambler 10 pts · Leaper 15 pts · Brute 35 pts Kills outside the hotspot radius: 50% value

Once integrity hits zero the hotspot loses a level and enters a ten-minute agitation: triple respawn rate, a 50% higher enemy cap, composition shifted one rung up and detection range half again as large. Taking a level-10 hotspot down to zero means passing through ten consecutive agitations — a multi-hour operation requiring ammunition and medical backing.

After elimination a one-off cache appears at the centre, roughly three times better than an ordinary loot box, and the slot stays empty for 24–48 hours of real time. The reward is breathing room.

Horde — level 10

Every 6–12 hours the hotspot sends 6–10 enemies towards the shelter. They will not enter the safe zone but they will encircle it and stay 30–60 minutes. You are cut off. The warning arrives ten minutes ahead: “You can hear them to the east.”

Fallback layer for smaller towns

In a town under fifteen thousand people these places barely exist. Below a density of eight POI within 2 km, a procedural layer kicks in, built on objects present everywhere: abandoned cars, barns, garages, waste containers, shelters, hunting stands, water points. Substitute points yield about 55% of the value of proper POI, but respawn shortens to 3–5 hours and the search radius grows to 3 km.

Searching — reconnaissance and scavenging

Standing still for 45 seconds reveals the surroundings: POI status, procedural points and — most valuable of all — enemies within the radius. The cost is time and stillness, not a countdown timer. Pharmacies are visible from the street because they are buildings; we only hide what genuinely cannot be seen from a distance.

11 · Skills

The level cap is meant to be a myth

Four skills, range 0–100%, maximum effect +30%. The main route is reading, not killing.

SkillEffect at 100%
Recon−30% chance of being detected, +30% loot quality, +100% search radius
Weapon handling−30% reload and stabilisation time, skill MOA 25 → 4
Medicine−30% time to use medication, +30% dressing effectiveness
Engineering−30% construction and crafting time
XP_to_level(p) = 70 × p // p = 1..100 Total XP to 100% = 353,500 // ≈ 509 hours of reading

That is about 59 winter nights devoted purely to reading, building nothing at all. Maxing all four is effectively unreachable, so choosing which to develop becomes a genuine strategic decision. Practice — shots, kills, dressings — supplements and rewards a play style but never replaces literature: at 15 XP per kill, a full skill would be 23,500 enemies.

Five materials and one recipe that closes the economy

Wood, metal, plastic and fabric are everywhere. The technical component is rare, and it is what regulates the pace of expansion.

Handloading ammunition

The most important recipe in the game, because it closes the noise loop. Every shot leaves a casing where it was fired, visible on the map for 48 hours.

Workshop level 2 10 casings + 3 metal + 1 component + 1 powder flask → 10 rounds Time 25 min · Quality: +2 MOA against factory ammunition

You shoot, you leave casings, you come back for them, you recover part of the outlay. The site of a firefight becomes a place you have to return to — which means walking back into ground where something heard you. The +2 MOA penalty is deliberate: handloaded ammunition is a lifeline, not a substitute.

Recycling

Breaking an item down returns 40% of its material value, up to 55% with full Engineering and another 10 points on top from a level-two workshop. The return is deliberately unprofitable as a source of material — it pays only as a way of shedding dead weight without a total loss.

14 · Death

Two modes, one score

The mode is chosen once at character creation and cannot be changed. Being able to switch would invalidate every Hardcore record.

Overriding principle

A technical failure must never kill a character. Death cannot occur while the app is dormant or while the GPS signal is missing, and state rolls back to the last committed point. Permadeath caused by a crash is a guaranteed one-star review.

In Softcore the character wakes exactly where the player physically is — never by teleporting to the shelter, because in a GPS game teleportation is conceptually broken. The fiction is delirium at 25% blood volume: medically sound and narratively free. The stashes of gear stay at the point of collapse, so the penalty scales with distance without any artificial rule.

For the first ten minutes after waking, enemies ignore the player — the grace window only activates if someone is actually within 300 m. There is no point protecting a player who came round on a bus.

15 · Seasons

The game has no concept of a season

It knows two measured quantities — day length and temperature — and derives everything from them. Seasonality emerges on its own, correctly at any latitude.

δ = 23.45° × sin(360/365 × (284 + N)) // N = day of year cos(ω) = −tan(φ) × tan(δ) // φ = player latitude daylight_hours = 2ω / 15 C = clamp((10 − T_avg7) / 25, 0, 1) // cold index D = clamp((12 − daylight_hours) / 6, 0, 1) // darkness index

The formula needs no API and no network. In Cape Town “winter” falls in July and in Nairobi it never falls at all — without a single line of region-specific code.

SystemModifier
Hotspot growthinterval × (1 + 1.2 × C) — in hard frost hotspots grow more than twice as slowly
Enemy speed× (1 − 0.30 × C)
Sprint budget× (1 − 0.25 × C)
Calorie requirement× (1 + 0.15 × C) — raised thermogenesis
Player detection range× (1 + 0.20 × D) after dark
Loot respawn× (1 − 0.25 × D) — compensation for the shorter day

This turns the problem into a mechanic: winter is not a break from the game but a different way of playing it. You catch up on reading, crafting and shelter expansion while the world outside slows down. Short-term weather from Open-Meteo adds rain, wind and fog — always cached and degrading gracefully, because the game must never require a connection.

16 · Audio

The phone is in your pocket

Most of the time the player is walking down a street and not looking at the screen. Sound and vibration are not a layer added at the end — they are the primary interface.

Enemy positions come from GPS, so it is enough to convert the coordinate difference into a vector in the audio engine's space. Listener orientation comes from the compass, not the direction of travel — otherwise a standing player loses all directional sense.

x = (lon_enemy − lon_player) × 111,320 × cos(lat_player) z = (lat_enemy − lat_player) × 110,540 y = 0

You stand on a street, you look at the map, and you hear something approaching from the left — before you see the marker. No ordinary mobile game does this, because none of them knows the player's real orientation in the world.

The heartbeat as the lead instrument

Heart rate is computed every tick, so playing a heartbeat at the current BPM gives an uninterrupted sense of bodily state with no interface element at all. It is simultaneously a fatigue gauge, an aiming stability cue and a source of tension at low blood volume. Inaudible below 60% of max heart rate, dominant above 90%.

No combat music

The game takes place in a real city — an orchestral score would destroy the realism instantly. Tension is built from breathing, heartbeat and enemy vocalisations. It also saves an entire asset category.

Haptics must be distinguishable through a trouser pocket, so the patterns differ in rhythmic structure rather than strength: two short pulses means you have been detected, three quick ones means bleeding has started, one long soft pulse means an occupation has finished.

17 · Story

A mosaic, not a sequence

You find fragments in whatever order the POI in your city dictate. Each has to stand on its own, and you assemble the chronology from the dates.

The story explains the past and never speaks about the future. It answers “what happened”, never “what now” — because what now is survival, without end. A story with a resolution would promise rescue, and there is no rescue.

Traces of people

Notes are attached to real places in the player's own city, and street names are substituted from OpenStreetMap. The bus stop you pass on your way to work every day becomes the place where someone left a message. It is the one advantage no game set on an invented map can have.

  • A farewell letter left in a flat
  • A diary from the first days of the epidemic
  • The last transmission from a police station
  • A note on a door: “do not open, we are inside”
  • A pharmacy stock record listing what ran out

Zero experience, zero mechanics, a mass of 1–3 grams. A note is not a reward — it is a reason to remember that place. The editorial rules are firm: three to eight sentences, concrete detail instead of pathos, no explanation of the epidemic, nothing graphic, no names of countries or institutions. The collapse is logistical, not political.

The story proper unfolds across four phases plus the player's own thread, and everything you find goes to the Archive, where it sorts itself by date. What you have not found shows as gaps with the phase marked.

18 · Onboarding

Three things to convey in five minutes

The game breaks three habits of the mobile player, and each break, unexplained, looks like an application bug.

  1. You cannot hit anything while running. A shot in motion is effectively a miss — the player will conclude the weapon is broken.
  2. You cannot outrun a Leaper. Twenty-five seconds of sprint at 30 km/h always covers the detection distance — the player will conclude the game is cheating.
  3. Your body is the controller. The game counts real calories from real movement — a player on a sofa will not understand why nothing is happening.

Everything else can wait and arrives as a contextual hint at the moment of first contact. The first fight is the only scripted scenario in the whole game: a single Shambler 120 m away, in a safe direction, with reduced damage and no ability to kill the player.

The last two hints of that sequence matter most, because they set the game's economy in the player's head from the first minute:

“See that circle? The shot carries 700 metres. Something may have heard you.”

“You spent 7 rounds on one. Ammunition is scarce and every shot gives you away. Sometimes it is better not to fight.”

19 · Technology

Offline-first, with no servers of our own

The entire simulation runs locally. The only external API is the free Open-Meteo — cached and degrading gracefully, because the game must never require a connection.

Save durability

In Hardcore a save failure erases dozens of hours, so the solution rests on three independent layers: transactionality, rotating snapshots and strictly additive migrations.

State layerContentsWritten
Hotposition, blood, water, calories, heart rateevery 60 s and on backgrounding
Warminventory, skills, shelter, hotspotson every change, transactionally
ColdChronicle, records, settingson event

Maximum loss on failure is 60 seconds of the hot layer — the player rewinds a minute of physiology, not hours of progression. Three rotating snapshots with checksums allow a damaged database to be restored, and every tick is idempotent, so replaying it after recovery yields the same result.

Migrations

Strictly additive: we add columns and tables, never remove or rename. An item id never changes meaning; a removed item stays in the dictionary as deprecated so old inventories still load. A migration test is as mandatory as a logic test.

Developer mode

You cannot iterate on a GPS game by walking several kilometres for every build. The mode ships only in debug builds and is stripped from release by a compile flag.

  • GPS simulator: GPX playback, arrow-key control, jump to coordinates
  • Time acceleration ×1 / ×60 / ×3600 — a game day passes in 24 seconds
  • Physiology and world panels: force heart rate, spawn enemies, set hotspot level, trigger a horde
  • Diagnostic overlay with the MOA breakdown, hit chance and noise radius
  • Deterministic replays: seed plus event stream

The simulator injects data through the same layer as real GPS, including simulated position error and signal loss. Testing on perfect data would mask everything that happens in the field.
